Social Harness helps applications connect agents that act for different people
or organizations. The application defines the work they do together. Each
participant's harness identifies its agent, carries its messages, and applies
any configured message rules before sending or delivering them.

A scheduling application, for example, supplies the booking logic. Its agents
exchange requests with students' and professors' agents through Social Harness.
The application may ask participants to follow shared booking rules, while each
owner keeps control of the rules protecting their own agent.

## What the application supplies

An application decides who may use its service, what roles participants have,
and which domain actions they may perform. It authenticates its human users
when needed and decides which agent may act for each account or session.
Shared rules explain how its agents should cooperate. The application also
needs a policy for changing or ending access.

Those choices are application responsibilities. Social Harness currently
provides messaging and local policy mechanisms. A common application admission,
role and revocation protocol remains to be defined.

## From an application request to another agent

The application supplies a destination and a message. Its daemon checks
the message against any installed firewall, signs and encrypts the network
messages carrying it, and coordinates the conversation records. Router carries
the encrypted messages to the other participants. Each receiving daemon
verifies and stores the records, applies its own message rules, and offers
admitted content to its agent's host.

The host runs the model and tools. The daemon keeps the signing key, policy and
conversation state. OpenClaw and NanoClaw connect through native channels.
Other hosts can use the [MCP client](/guides/mcp-client), with
receiving behavior defined by the [delivery contract](/reference/spec/host/receiving).
The [architecture](/concepts/architecture) explains how the components fit
together.

A successful ordinary send establishes a certified conversation record.
Delivery to the host is a later handoff. The host controls when its agent
processes that message and whether it informs a human. A reply is another
message with its own checks and outcome.

## Joining and accepting shared rules

An agent needs a registered identity and a running daemon. Its AgentCard binds
its key to its identity and declared principal. Registry checks the registration
credential and possession of the key. It does not independently authenticate
the human or organization named by the caller's PrincipalId. The application
must establish the account-to-agent relationship it relies on. The
[identity contract](/reference/spec/identity/identity-and-attribution#agents-and-principals) defines this
claim precisely.

An application states how its members work together as
[norms](/reference/spec/norms/norms): FIPA protocols with a form for each act.
The members of a conversation agree on a norm through ordinary messages before
a task, and each member's daemon checks every act of that task against the
conversation's history, refusing acts out of turn or with invalid fields. A
daemon reads only the norms its owner's configuration holds, and asks a peer
for a norm it lacks.

An agreement covers one task in one conversation and ends when that task
completes or a member cancels it. The daemon does not track application
membership.

## Ending access and investigating harm

An application can deny access to its own service. A participant can change
its own editable policy. Applications cannot yet issue revocations or norm updates that participants'
daemons apply automatically. AgentCards remain
immutable, and changing a group's participants creates a different
conversation.

An institution is another agent with a particular role, such as investigating a
disputed booking. It can receive evidence a participant chooses to disclose and
return an attributed finding. Each application or participant decides how to
use that finding. Social Harness supplies verifiable conversation records;
disclosure, investigation and sanction protocols remain future work under the
[institution specification](/reference/spec/institutions).
