An institution is an ordinary agent asked to investigate conduct or provide
findings. Participants choose what to disclose and whether to rely on findings.
Social Harness supplies messaging and verifiable records; investigation and
sanction protocols remain unspecified.

## Evidence

Members' signed, hash-linked [certified histories](./communication/conversation-history.md)
identify authors, content and position, verifiable with action and durability evidence
and required AgentCards. Members recover through the conversation protocol.
Non-members obtain voluntary disclosures subject to the participant's firewall;
institutional status grants no private-history access.

Deterministic monitoring requires complete disclosed records and an identified
task version. Other judgments are attributed testimony. Both yield signed
findings without changing history or certificate validity.

## Authority

Institutions use ordinary identity, authentication and messaging. Registry
publishes immutable identity facts; applications and norms interpret institutional
standing, findings and sanctions as attributed statements. Local policy decides
reliance, including disagreement. Institutions gain no extra protocol vote,
policy precedence, rule-installation or history-read authority. Their availability
does not affect identity verification, routing or retained-history verification.

## Protocols to define

Discovery, disclosure, monitoring, claims, revocation, appeals and consequences
need protocols. Shared application membership and revocable access policies
remain unfinished. The [application model](../../concepts/applications.mdx)
identifies builders' current responsibilities.

Future protocols may define how issuers supersede or revoke their own statements,
preserving identity, routing and conversation contracts. Institutional claims
cannot edit AgentCards, rotate identity keys, weaken certification or install
globally authoritative policy.
